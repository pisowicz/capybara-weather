      How This Forecast Is Made — Multi-Model Blend

      The Weather Channel doesn't rely on a single weather model. Its forecast engine ingests 100+ numerical models (GFS, ECMWF, its own IBM GRAF, and more), bias-corrects each one against recent observations, and blends them into a single consensus point forecast. This panel replicates that pipeline at miniature scale: three real global models, a skill-weighted blend, and — when nearby stations are reporting — a live observation correction on top.

      The Real Pipeline

      1Observe

      Satellites, radar, aircraft, balloons, and 250k+ personal weather stations feed current conditions.

      2Model

      Dozens of NWP models — including IBM GRAF running the MPAS core at ~3 km globally, updated hourly — simulate the atmosphere.

      3Blend & Correct

      A DiCast-derived engine bias-corrects every model against recent verification and computes optimal weights per location and lead time.

      4Localize

      Forecasts are interpolated to a fine grid, then corrected against nearby surface stations — exactly what the Nearby Stations feature does here.

      Read the full research in docs/HOW_TWC_BUILDS_FORECASTS.md.
